Types of Mental Health Problems

A calm, clear guide to common mental health problems — what they are, how they can be treated, and where to find support.

Click each item to read a short explanation and suggestions for support. This page is informational and not a substitute for professional help.

Anger

What it is: A natural emotion that can become harmful when frequent or intense.

How to cope: Pause before reacting, practice breathing/grounding, talk therapies and anger-management techniques can help.

Anxiety and panic attacks

What it is: Excessive worry or sudden intense fear (panic attacks).

Support: Cognitive-behavioural therapy (CBT), relaxation techniques, medication where appropriate, and support from friends/family.

Bipolar disorder

What it is: Mood swings between lows (depression) and highs (mania/hypomania).

Support: Specialist assessment, mood-stabilising medication, therapy, routine and support from loved ones.

Body dysmorphia (BDD)

What it is: Excessive worry about perceived flaws in appearance.

Support: CBT for BDD, specialist clinics, self-help strategies and compassionate support from friends/family.

Borderline personality disorder (BPD)

What it is: Emotional instability, difficulties in relationships and self-image.

Support: Specialist therapies (e.g. DBT), structured support and guidance for family and friends.

Depression

What it is: Persistent low mood, loss of interest, and other emotional and physical symptoms.

Support: Talk therapies, medication when needed, lifestyle changes and social support.

Dissociation and dissociative disorders

What it is: Feeling disconnected from yourself or surroundings; can follow trauma.

Support: Trauma-informed therapy and grounding/self-care techniques; support networks.

Eating problems

What it is: Difficulties with eating, body image and control around food.

Support: Specialist eating-disorder services, therapy, nutrition support and family involvement.

Hearing voices

What it is: Experiencing auditory perceptions others do not hear; can be part of several conditions.

Support: Talk with professionals, voice-hearing groups, coping strategies and compassionate support.

Hoarding

What it is: Difficulty discarding possessions, creating clutter and distress.

Support: Specialist community services, CBT approaches, practical help and family support.

Hypomania and mania

What it is: Periods of very high mood, energy and activity that can be risky.

Support: Professional assessment, medication and regular routines to manage risks.

Loneliness

What it is: A painful sense of isolation or lack of social connection.

Support: Reach out to groups, befriending services, local community and small social steps.

Introduction to mental health problems

What it is: Overview of causes, types of help and how to find support.

Support: See local services, GP, charities and trusted information resources.

Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)

What it is: Recurrent intrusive thoughts and repetitive behaviours performed to reduce distress.

Support: CBT (exposure & response prevention), medication and specialist services.

Panic attacks

What it is: Sudden periods of intense fear with physical symptoms (racing heart, breathlessness).

Support: Grounding/breathing techniques, CBT, and medical support if needed.

Paranoia

What it is: Persistent mistrust or suspicion of others without clear reason.

Support: Professional assessment, therapy and social support can help.

Personality disorder

What it is: Patterns of thinking and behaviour that cause distress and interfere with relationships.

Support: Specialist therapies, structured support and education for families.

Phobias

What it is: Intense fear of specific objects or situations.

Support: CBT (exposure), relaxation skills and brief therapy programmes.

Postnatal depression & perinatal mental health

What it is: Depression or other mental health problems around pregnancy and after birth.

Support: Specialist perinatal services, GP referral, support groups and family help.

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)

What it is: Ongoing distress after trauma, including flashbacks and avoidance.

Support: Trauma-focused therapies, specialist services and peer support.

Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD)

What it is: Severe mood symptoms linked to the menstrual cycle.

Support: Medical assessment, symptom-tracking, lifestyle approaches and treatment options.

Psychosis

What it is: Experiences such as hallucinations or delusions that affect perception of reality.

Support: Early intervention services, medication, therapy and family support.

Recreational drugs, alcohol and addiction

What it is: Substance use affecting mental health and functioning.

Support: Addiction services, harm reduction, detox where needed and ongoing support.

Schizoaffective disorder

What it is: A combination of mood disorder symptoms and psychosis.

Support: Specialist psychiatric care, medication and psychosocial support.

Schizophrenia

What it is: A long-term condition affecting thought, perception and behaviour.

Support: Early intervention, medication, therapy and family support services.

Seasonal affective disorder (SAD)

What it is: Depression linked to seasonal changes (often winter).

Support: Light therapy, CBT, medication and lifestyle adjustments.

Self-esteem

What it is: How we value ourselves; something that can be improved with practice.

Support: Self-compassion practices, therapy and achievable goal-setting.

Self-harm

What it is: Deliberate self-injury to cope with distress.

Support: Immediate safety planning, crisis support, therapy and trusted contacts.

Sleep problems

What it is: Insomnia and other difficulties with sleep.

Support: Sleep hygiene, CBT for insomnia, medical review where needed.

Stress

What it is: Pressure or strain that affects body and mind.

Support: Time-management, relaxation, and seeking support when needed.

Suicidal thoughts

What it is: Thoughts about ending life — a serious sign to seek immediate help.

Support: Contact crisis services, trusted people, health professionals and emergency help.

Suicide attempt — how to cope after

What it is: Guidance for recovery after a suicide attempt.

Support: Follow-up care, emotional support, practical help and specialist services.

Tardive dyskinesia

What it is: Movement disorder sometimes caused by long-term medication.

Support: Medical review, medication changes and neurological assessment.

Trauma

What it is: Deeply distressing experiences that affect long-term mental health.

Support: Trauma-focused therapy, safety planning and trusted support networks.

Where to get support

  • Speak to your GP or local health provider
  • Contact crisis lines if you are at immediate risk
  • Look for specialist services, charities and peer support groups
  • Trusted friends and family can provide vital support
